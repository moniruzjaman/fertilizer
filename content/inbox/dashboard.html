Section 01

Executive Summary

Bangladesh's agricultural sector confronts a defining paradox: over 1.365 million tonnes of fertilizer sit in government warehouses, yet farmers in Rajshahi, Jashore, Kurigram and Lalmonirhat face acute shortages — paying 50–100% above official prices. The crisis is not one of supply, but of distribution integrity, institutional accountability and subsidy targeting.

This report diagnoses root causes; inventories the digital assets already at our disposal (Krishak Card, BDS/EDLMS, NID, the 2026 Dealer Policy); distills actionable lessons from four Asian comparators; and presents a phased, technically feasible and politically urgent reform roadmap aligned with the SDGs, FAO's "Four Betters", and the nationalist, farmer-first ethos of Bir Uttam President Ziaur Rahman.

The current system is fiscally unsustainable, environmentally harmful, and socially regressive. Reform pays for itself within 2–3 fiscal cycles.

I · The Burden

Fiscal hemorrhage (Tk 17,001 Cr), regressive capture by the top 20%, environmental decay, >70% import dependency, dealer collusion.

II · The Turning Point

Digital assets ready, regional lessons learned, the 2026 Comprehensive Dealer Policy enacted, KrishiChain pilot designed.

III · The Bloom

Precision agriculture, farmer dignity, environmental recovery, sovereign food security, agricultural diplomacy leadership.

Core Recommendation

Transition from a blanket, dealer-centric subsidy model to a biometrically authenticated, land-linked, digitally tracked distribution system that ensures the right farmer receives the right input, at the right price, at the right time — saving Tk 2,000–3,000 crore annually through leakage reduction alone.

The High-Use Paradox — Asia's Warning

China credited nitrogen fertilizers with up to 50% of yield gains; India's nitrogen consumption exploded from 0.21 Mt (1960–61) to 17.63 Mt (2018–19). The price: NUE collapsed from 61%→50% (China) and 50%→42% (India), while India now spends >US$8 billion annually on subsidies. Bangladesh must pursue sustainable intensification — optimizing the whole farming system, not maximizing a single input.

Lessons from Asia: What Works, What Fails

The Biometric DBT Revolution

  • Aadhaar-enabled PoS authenticates farmers at retail shops — subsidies released only after verified sales
  • 2.87 lakh retailers serve 8.4 crore farmers on a "no denial" basis (since 2018)
  • e-Bill system links payments to verified dealer sales records
  • Piloting land & crop-based pre-booking limits via app
  • Fiscal burden remains vast: ₹1.71 lakh crore budgeted 2026–27; >US$8B/yr
Lesson for Bangladesh

Biometric authentication at point of sale is non-negotiable for eliminating diversion. The shift from blanket subsidies to land-linked quotas is the next frontier — even a deeply ossified system can be reformed with robust tech infrastructure.

Zero Growth & Digital Countryside

  • 2015: removed manufacturing subsidies; launched "Action Plan for Zero Growth in Chemical Fertilizer Use" (target 2020)
  • Targets: fertilizer utilization >40%; soil testing coverage >90%
  • 99%+ of villages have fiber/4G; ~80% have farmer information societies
  • Internet-informed farmers 3.5% more fertilizer-efficient
  • E-commerce operators cut chemical use to protect product reputation; STFFT & organic replacement (OFRT) scaled nationally
Lesson for Bangladesh

Reducing subsidies must be paired with massive investment in physical infrastructure ("high-standard farmland") and technical support. Digital infrastructure must precede or accompany subsidy reform. Market-based incentives can drive behavior change more effectively than input subsidies alone.

Precision & Low-Emission Rice

  • "One Must, Five Reductions": certified seeds while reducing seeding rates, fertilizers, pesticides, water, post-harvest losses
  • Mekong Delta 1 Million Hectare High-Quality, Low-Emission Rice Program
  • Achieved 31.3% average reduction in fertilizer use; 20% cut in nitrogen fertilizer & irrigation water
  • Soil health cards deliver tailored recommendations; Red River Delta shows cost of decades of over-application
Lesson for Bangladesh

Precision agriculture and organic integration can simultaneously improve farmer economics and environmental resilience. Reduction is not synonymous with loss — targeted packages for specific value chains drive meaningful change.

Cooperatives & Climate-Smart Models

  • AMIAnan Farmers Entrepreneur Agriculture Cooperative (AFEAC): aggregating demand, delivering extension, improving incomes
  • PHP 5,000 cash subsidies to 600,000 rice farmers
  • Climate-Smart Coconut-Based Farming Systems for diversification
  • Caution: inefficient subsidy reach, low NUE, Laguna Lake water pollution from historical overuse
Lesson for Bangladesh

Cooperatives are powerful intermediaries — but without digital tracking, subsidy schemes remain prone to leakage. Cash transfers can be more efficient than physical input distribution.

Three-Pillar Strategic Framework

Pillar I — Modernize Subsidies

  • • e-Bill system (India model) — payments linked to verified sales
  • • Biometric PoS authentication
  • • Land-linked quotas from BDS/EDLMS parcels
  • • Blockchain verification ledger
  • • Shift from subsidizing inputs to subsidizing verified outcomes

Pillar II — Build Institutions

  • • Nationwide Soil Health Card program (China STFFT model)
  • • Village-level Agro-Service Centres — inputs, advice, machinery, credit in one hub
  • • Farmer cooperative professionalization (AFEAC model)
  • • Extension service reform — trusted, site-specific advisors
  • • Regulatory enforcement capacity building

Pillar III — Leverage Digital & Markets

  • • National digital agriculture platform (mobile-centric advisory)
  • • Market linkages rewarding sustainably produced goods
  • • Inclusive design: voice UI, USSD — closing the digital divide
  • • AI/ML demand forecasting & early warning
  • • Voluntary land consolidation/leasing for economies of scale

The Bloom

সমৃদ্ধি: কৃষকের মর্যাদা, সার্বভৌম খাদ্য নিরাপত্তা

A future of precision agriculture, farmer dignity, environmental recovery and sovereign food security

The Farmer's Experience

A farmer in Bhurungamari walks to the authorized dealer, places her thumb on the biometric scanner, and receives her exact entitlement of urea and DAP — calculated from her soil test results and crop declaration — at the government-fixed price. The transaction takes under 10 minutes. No queue. No black market. No uncertainty.

The System's Reality

The Ministry tracks every kilogram from import/production to final farmer sale on a national digital ledger. Policy becomes evidence-based, not reactive. The subsidy bill declines as efficiency improves. Nitrate runoff falls, soils recover, and the Padma-Meghna basins heal. The environment recovers.

A warehouse full of fertilizer means nothing if the farmer's hands remain empty. This reform honors that legacy through three nationalist imperatives:

Biometric PoS, blockchain ledger and public dashboards hold officer, dealer and minister accountable to the farmer.

Empowerment, Not Dependency

Land-linked, soil-tested entitlements treat farmers as rational stewards — not recipients of charity.

🇧🇩

Self-Reliance

Reviving domestic production and leveraging our own digital rails builds a sovereign, future-proof food system.
